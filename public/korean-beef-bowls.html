Pantry Check

Standing Kit vs. Per-Batch

The sauce is built entirely from shelf-stable staples — soy, sesame oil, ginger, red pepper, black pepper, brown sugar. Stock those once and they cover this recipe indefinitely.

What you actually shop for each time: ground beef and fresh aromatics. Everything else is already on the shelf. That's what makes this viable as a repeat meal-prep dish rather than a project.

Method
  1. Brown the ground beef and minced garlic together in a large skillet over medium heat, breaking the meat into crumbles as it cooks. Go until no pink remains, then drain the grease.
  2. While that cooks, whisk the sauce in a small bowl: brown sugar, soy sauce, sesame oil, ginger, red pepper flakes, and black pepper.
  3. Pour the sauce over the beef and let it simmer a minute or two until it tightens up and coats.
  4. Serve over hot rice. Green onions and sesame seeds on top.

Your Version

Gochujang Base, Less Soy

The way you used to make it, and the better version: swap in gochujang and cut the soy sauce back. Gochujang carries its own salt and sugar, so running full soy on top of full brown sugar stacks salty on sweet and flattens everything out.

Starting point: 1–2 Tbsp gochujang, soy reduced from 1/4 cup to about 3 Tbsp. Taste before it finishes simmering — gochujang brands vary a lot in heat and salt.

Use tamari over standard soy. Smoother and less sharp, which lets the sesame and ginger actually come through instead of getting buried. Reduced-sodium is the other must — full-strength soy makes this too salty and too sweet at the same time.

Fresh ginger, cut into ½-inch matchsticks — not grated, not powdered. Matchsticks stay intact through the simmer and give you distinct bites of ginger throughout the bowl rather than a flat background note. Fresh ginger is already in the Wegmans cart.

Upgrades From The Comments
Double the sauce most common note
Multiple people run a double batch of sauce against a single pound of beef for more flavor and better rice coverage. Worth trying on your first run back.
Gochugaru alongside the red pepper flakes you already own this
One reviewer used both Korean pepper flakes and standard crushed red pepper. Given you have gochugaru sitting upstairs, this is a free upgrade that pushes it more authentically Korean.
Fresh ginger instead of dried in your cart
Grated fresh ginger in place of the 1/4 tsp ground. You've got fresh ginger coming in the Wegmans order — noticeably brighter than the jar.
Cornstarch slurry to thicken
About 1/2 tsp cornstarch dissolved in water, stirred in at the end. Tightens the sauce so it clings to the beef instead of running into the rice.
Broccoli in with the sauce solves your greens
Blanched or frozen broccoli added at the sauce stage turns this into a complete one-bowl meal. You keep frozen broccoli stocked constantly — microwave it and fold it in.
Kimchi on the side, or romaine wraps
Kimchi's tang cuts the sweet-savory sauce well. Others skip the rice entirely and wrap the beef in romaine leaves for a lower-carb version — less relevant while bulking, but useful to know.
Storage
Fridge
3–4 days in airtight containers. Store beef and rice together or separate — both reheat fine.
Freezer
The beef mixture freezes well for 2–3 months. Portion flat in bags or containers so it thaws fast.
Reheat
Microwave straight from the fridge, covered, stirring once. Splash of water if the rice has dried out.
Prep note
Cook the rice in a separate batch — a 3× run needs 6 cups cooked, which is more than one Anyday batch. Rice holds fine in the fridge for the week.
